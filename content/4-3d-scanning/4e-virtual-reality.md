Although Unearthing the Legacy of Islamic Spain closed its doors in January 2025, the exhibition remains open here — permanently, and to anyone in the world. Using Matterport, a 3D capture technology that combines panoramic photography with spatial depth mapping, the team recorded a complete digital twin of the exhibition gallery at the Meadows Museum. Matterport works by scanning a space from dozens of positions and stitching those captures into a seamless, navigable three-dimensional model: the result is not a video or a slideshow but a virtual environment you can move through at your own pace, as if walking the gallery floor yourself.

In the embedded tour below, visitors can navigate freely through the exhibition — moving from room to room, turning to take in the installation from any angle, and approaching the capital to examine it in its display context, surrounded by the nineteenth-century prints, drawings, paintings, and books with which it was originally in dialogue. Interactive tags embedded throughout the space invite deeper exploration: clicking on objects and wall labels opens additional information about each work, so that the tour functions not merely as a record of the exhibition but as an annotated, self-guided visit. For researchers, the capture also preserves something exhibitions rarely leave behind — a complete spatial document of curatorial choices, sightlines, and juxtapositions. All Matterport content and edits were produced by Anna Shovlin, the project's Technology Lead and 3D Imaging Specialist (UT Dallas).

The Matterport tour is only the first step in the project's virtual ambitions. The team is exploring further VR integration using standard headset platforms, with one experience in particular on the horizon: placing the capital back where it began. A virtual reconstruction of the Salón Rico — the sumptuous reception hall of Madinat al-Zahrāʾ — would allow visitors to encounter the capital not in a museum vitrine but in architectural context, atop a column amid the polychrome carving and processional spaces of the caliphal court. VR integration remains in the planning stages; this section will be updated as the work develops — one of the ways this publication, like the project itself, continues to grow.
