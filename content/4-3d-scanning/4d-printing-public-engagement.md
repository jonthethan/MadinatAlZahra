## 4d — 3D Printing and Public Engagement
▸	A 3D-printed replica of the capital has been produced in multiple versions, enabling hands-on engagement in the museum gallery and educational settings. Initial prototypes were printed using fused deposition modeling (FDM) printers with 1.75mm ABS filament at SMU’s Deason Innovation Gym (DIG) and UT Dallas’s 3D printing lab. The capital was printed in five interlocking segments assembled with Velcro for easy disassembly. Higher-fidelity reproductions were subsequently produced using the 3D Systems ZPrinter 450, which builds forms from a gypsum-based composite powder bound by a starch-based inkjet binder; after printing, the fragile pieces are treated with ZBond (cyanoacrylate) to increase strength and produce a smooth, slightly glossy surface resembling marble. These prints were produced in collaboration with Dr. Matthew Boulanger, Archeology Research Collections (ARC), SMU.
▸	Tactile access: The printed replica allows visitors, including those with visual impairments, to experience the capital's form through touch — a significant accessibility dimension.
▸	Classroom use: The replica and 3D model are being integrated into art history and archaeology courses at SMU and UT Dallas.
